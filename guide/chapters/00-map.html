The map

Math Finder takes a theorem you write in LaTeX, turns it into a precise Lean statement, searches for a proof, checks every step with Lean (a program that cannot be fooled), and explains the result back to you. You can give hints at any point, so it works like a partner rather than a black box.

How the pieces connect

The proof loop in one paragraph

The planner (an LLM) suggests a proof plan: a few small lemmas plus how they combine. The critic asks Lean whether the plan even fits together, then checks each lemma: proved, false (Lean found a counterexample), or stuck. The prover attacks each lemma, first with cheap automatic tactics, then with LLM-written proofs, repairing them from Lean's error messages. Anything stuck goes back to the planner as specific feedback, or to you as a question. Once everything is proved, the optimizer looks for a shorter proof and the explainer writes it up in plain math.
