M4d: When a lemma is stuck, search for it

What and why. Sometimes a plan is right but one of its lemmas is still too big: the hammer fails, and Claude's proofs keep failing even after repair rounds. Until now that lemma just became feedback ("lemma X got stuck"). A better move is the one a mathematician makes: treat the stuck lemma as a new, smaller theorem and plan a proof for it. That's recursive decomposition. The search calls itself on the stuck lemma (a sub-search). If the sub-search finds a proof, its helper lemmas are spliced into the parent plan and the parent carries on. MAX_DEPTH = 1 stops this after one level, so a hard problem can't spawn searches forever.

  1. Teach the search to call itself. Replace the whole file:

    {{file:mathfinder/search.py@m4d}}

    What changed:

    • search takes a new depth argument (0 for your theorem, 1 inside a sub-search), and every event now carries "depth" so watchers can show sub-searches indented. A sub-search skips step 0 (the direct hammer), because the prover just ran the hammer on that same statement.
    • prove_plan uses a while loop instead of for: a sub-search can insert lemmas into the list being walked, and changing a list while a for loop runs over it skips or repeats items. With an explicit index i we decide exactly where to continue. It also gets on_event, hints and depth so it can start the sub-search with the same watchers and hints.
    • A function calling itself (here search → prove_plan → search) is recursion. It always needs a stopping rule, and ours is depth < MAX_DEPTH.
    • splice renames each sub-lemma to <stuck lemma>_<name> (so a sub-lemma step of odd_sum becomes odd_sum_step) and rewrites every mention of it. rf"\b{re.escape(old)}\b" is a regular expression for the whole word only: \b is a word boundary, so renaming step leaves steps alone, and re.escape stops symbols in the name being read as regex syntax. plan.lemmas[i:i] = inserted is slice assignment: it inserts a whole list at position i. The stuck lemma's proof becomes the sub-search's main proof.

With the real model you'll see a sub-search on the command line as lines like ↳ odd_sum is stuck: starting a sub-search on it followed by indented sub-plan and lemma lines.

Common errors

  • RecursionError or a search that never ends: the depth < MAX_DEPTH check is missing, or you pass depth instead of depth + 1 to the sub-search.
  • The final check fails with unknown identifier 'step': splice didn't rename inside the stuck lemma's new proof (rename(sub.main_proof)).
  • Lemmas get proved twice or skipped: prove_plan must do i += splice(...) and then the normal i += 1.
